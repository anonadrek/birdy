Birdy on Instagram & Facebook

One bird every morning

Each species page that goes live on birdy.community gets one post at 08:00, built from one template and filled in from the checked species record. All posts are in English. Here are four templates: three for the daily post, and a song quiz video to add later for Reels and TikTok. Pick one for the daily post.

Example content: the Great Tit, all the way down.

Why it works
It reads as Birdy even as a thumbnail: the same paper band, with the name in the same place, every day. The profile grid turns into a field guide you can scroll, and the name never sits on the photo, so it stays legible whatever the picture.
What it needs
One HTML template rendered to a 1080 × 1350 PNG with Playwright (already in the website’s toolchain), the running number from publishing order, and one short handwritten line: “Listen for” plus the page’s voice fact, else “Look for” plus the first field mark, kept under about 45 characters. The italic accent is the last word of the English name (Tit, Blackbird); long names step down a size.
CC BY-SA photos
No crop, no tear and no text on the photo: it sits whole on the paper like a mounted print, inside the grid’s 3:4 safe zone, with the band below (the Blackbird above). Still clearly the same card.
Effort
Low. About a day for the template, the BY-SA variant and the render step.
B

Option B · carousel, 3 slides

Three-slide carousel

A mini field guide to swipe through: the bird, how to know it, and when to look for it in Sweden.

3 images · 4:5Effort mediumGrid: shows slide 1
Why it works
The most useful post: slides 2 and 3 are field-guide pages people save. Instagram can show a carousel a second time starting at slide 2, so one post gets two chances in the feed.
What it needs
Three templates plus the month chart, drawn from the same monthly Artportalen shares the species page charts (shown here with an illustrative shape). Slide 2 uses a fixed centred zoom on the photo, so there is no per-species placement. A red-list line (“Swedish Red List 2025: Near Threatened (NT)”) appears on slide 3 only for red-listed species; the Great Tit has none. Facebook shows the three images as an album, not a swipe.
CC BY-SA photos
Slide 1 becomes A’s mounted variant; slide 2 drops the photo window and sets the field marks on paper only.
Effort
Medium. About two days: three templates, the chart and the BY-SA variants.

Option C · single image

Full bleed

The photo fills the frame, with the name and one line in white over a dark fade. The cleanest and most Instagram-native of the four.

Great Tit

Why it works
The photo does the work and the feed looks calm and photographic, the way bird accounts usually look on Instagram.
What it needs
A 4:5 crop from photos that are mostly 3:2: the Great Tit keeps 1,280 of its 2,400 pixels of width, and a centred crop can cut tails and beaks. Each species needs a stored focal point or a quick crop check. White text also depends on the dark fade, which darkens the lower third of every photo.
CC BY-SA photos
The fade and the text can’t go on them. The 13 species without a CC0, public domain or CC BY photo get the uncropped photo on espresso with the text below, which breaks the otherwise uniform grid (the Blackbird tile in the grid below).
Effort
Low to build, about half a day, plus a crop check per species. Weakest Birdy signature of the three: it could be any bird account.
D

Option D · vertical video · later

Song quiz

A 15-second vertical video: hear the song, guess, then see the bird. Not for the daily post at launch, but a second template for Reels and TikTok once the daily post runs. Storyboard only.

Later, second template9:16 · 15 sEffort high
Why it works
Reels and TikTok favour short video with sound, and a quiz gives people a reason to watch to the end. It shows sound ID, the feature that is hardest to explain in a still image.
What it needs
The species page’s recording (already trimmed and checked against Birdy’s BirdNET), ffmpeg to build the video (already in the pipeline), text burned in for people watching on mute, and video upload through Meta’s and TikTok’s APIs.
Licences
Photos follow the same rules; a BY-SA photo shows whole in the reveal frame with the name below. Setting a recording to video always counts as an adaptation under CC 4.0, so only CC0, public domain or CC BY recordings, credited “edited”.
Effort
High. Three to four days; build it once the daily post is running.

House rules

Photos

Only photos whose licence lets us post them, filtered in the code that picks the photo, not by hand. The picker prefers CC0, public domain and CC BY: 167 of the 180 species pages have at least one. The Great Tit example is CC0.

Licence URLs are written as plain text because links in Instagram captions aren’t clickable. Format: Photo: {author}, {licence} ({licence URL}), via Wikimedia Commons, cropped.

Facts

Everything in the image and the caption is filled in from the checked species page: the lead’s first sentence, the field marks, the Artportalen months, the red-list status and the look-alike. No new claims, no AI-written extras, no model calls. Only the template words (“Know it by”, “When & where”, “Listen for”) are fixed text.

Posting

Once a day at 08:00 Stockholm time, the same time as the app’s Bird of the Day notification, in publishing order, so the running number counts up. On Facebook the link is clickable in the post. On Instagram the caption says “link in bio”, and the bio links to a birdy.community page with this week’s species. TikTok joins with option D.
